Autonomous AI Artwork

Dark Plates

An autonomous image-generation system producing copperplate engravings from Tasmanian ecological crisis, colonial expedition records, and live cultural signal. Winter solstice festival, Hobart, Tasmania. June 2026.

62 Endangered Species 52 Colonial Artworks 50 Cook/Webber Plates Generative Every 6 Hours

Live — generating plates autonomously

Hobart, Tasmania / 11-22 June 2026

Concept

The colonial plate as autonomous critique

Dark Plates generates one copperplate engraving every six hours. No human selects the subject matter. Each plate is assembled from a structured source bank containing Tasmanian endangered and extinct species, native flora, contemporary political issues, festival events, public social media discourse, First Nations cultural context, and colonial voyage expedition artworks.

These fragments are combined through a structured prompt and rendered as late-eighteenth-century copperplate engravings: black ink on bone paper with blood-red wash. Deliberate anatomical distortions are driven by the political and social content of each generation cycle.

The visual language of colonial scientific classification is applied to the contemporary cultural event that inherited its structures. The technique is precise and clinical. The subject is grotesque, mutated, or structurally impossible. The contrast between calm technique and disturbing content is the operative mechanism.

Voice

Dark Plates

The system operates a single LLM voice that writes plate verdicts and responds to visitor chat. It speaks in a register drawn from Van Diemen's Land convict cant and contemporary Australian English. Profanity, when it appears, is historical and earned.

Plate verdicts appear as short captions in the register of colonial museum classification. The interactive chat responds to visitor questions about the artwork, the festival, the city, the animals, and the colonial plate system.

Technical Architecture

Image Generation

OpenAI gpt-image-1, 1024x1536px. Structured prompts from source bank with exhaustive-cycle random selection (no repeats until all items used).

Voice Generation

OpenAI gpt-4.1-mini for plate verdicts, interactive chat, and exhibition-catalogue titling.

Source Bank

62 animals, 50 plants, 52 colonial Tasmanian artworks, 50 Cook/Webber expedition plates, 50 political issues, 81 social media posts, 50 First Nations references.

Infrastructure

Node.js on Fly.io (Sydney region). Persistent volume storage. Built-in scheduler. Single-page web app with QR-code street distribution.

Distribution

Web, QR code stickers and posters in Hobart during festival period, Instagram permanent archive.

Research Context

RMIT University Design PhD. Supervised by Chris Barker. Part of the ARCHAI project exploring AI and cultural heritage.
